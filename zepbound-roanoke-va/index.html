    How Zepbound Treatment Works in Roanoke, VA

    How Zepbound Works in Roanoke

    Tirzepatide acts on two gut hormone receptors. GIP and GLP-1 together help regulate appetite, slow how fast the stomach empties and improve the way the body handles blood sugar after a meal. Most people feel full on smaller portions. It is injected under the skin of the stomach, thigh or upper arm once a week.

    Who Zepbound Is For

    Zepbound is approved for adults with a BMI of 30 or higher. Adults with a BMI of 27 or more also qualify if they have a condition tied to weight, such as high blood pressure, type 2 diabetes, high cholesterol or sleep apnea. A separate approval covers moderate to severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity. It is not approved for children.

    Zepbound Dosing Schedule in Roanoke

    Everyone starts at 2.5 mg. That first month is about letting your stomach adjust, not about weight loss, so modest early changes are normal. After four weeks the dose usually moves up in 2.5 mg steps to 5, 7.5, 10, 12.5 and up to 15 mg, with at least four weeks at each. Maintenance doses are typically 5, 10 or 15 mg.

    What to Expect Week by Week in Roanoke

    The first month is mostly quiet. You may notice a smaller appetite within the first week, and stomach upset tends to cluster in the day or two after a shot or a dose increase. In the SURMOUNT-1 trial, adults on the highest dose lost about 20 percent of body weight on average over 72 weeks. Individual results vary.

    Zepbound Side Effects

    Nausea and diarrhea come up most. Vomiting, constipation, indigestion, stomach pain and redness at the injection site are also common, especially during the first days after each dose increase. Serious but less common risks include pancreatitis, gallbladder problems, kidney injury from dehydration and severe allergic reactions. Zepbound carries a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents.

    Eligibility and Screening in Roanoke

    Your provider starts with height, weight and BMI. They then go through your history and medications, looking hardest at insulin, glipizide-type drugs and oral contraceptives. Anyone with medullary thyroid carcinoma or MEN 2 in their family is not a candidate, and Zepbound is not used while pregnant. Labs may be requested.

    Zepbound Compared With Related Options in Roanoke

    Zepbound and Mounjaro are the same medicine. Zepbound carries the weight management label and Mounjaro carries the type 2 diabetes label, which matters for insurance and for how your provider writes the prescription. A direct comparison trial found larger average weight loss with tirzepatide than with Wegovy. Compounded tirzepatide is not FDA-approved and is a different product from Zepbound.
